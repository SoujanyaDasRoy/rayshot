# RayShot Vision

## 1. Vision

**A world where anyone can create films, regardless of the hardware they can afford or their technical experience.**

> **Your laptop shouldn't decide whether you're allowed to create.**

RayShot exists to reduce the technical and hardware barriers between a person's creative idea and a finished piece of video.

## 2. The Problem

Modern video editing has an accessibility gap.

Beginners and aspiring filmmakers often face two barriers at the same time:

1. **Software complexity:** professional non-linear editors are powerful but can require substantial learning before a new user feels productive.
2. **Hardware requirements:** high-resolution media, effects, decoding, caching, and rendering can overwhelm lower-cost machines.

Simple editors reduce friction, but users may outgrow them as their ambitions increase. Professional editors provide power, but the learning and hardware requirements can become a barrier to continued creation.

RayShot aims to occupy the space between those extremes.

## 3. Product Belief

RayShot is not intended to make filmmaking less creative. It is intended to remove unnecessary technical friction around filmmaking.

The product should hide complexity where complexity does not create creative value, while preserving control where creative decisions matter.

The guiding principle is:

> **Simplify the tools, not the creativity.**

## 4. Product Positioning

RayShot is a capable video editor for budding filmmakers and hobbyists who want more control than simple editors provide but do not want the complexity and hardware burden of traditional professional NLEs.

RayShot should be:

- approachable for first-time NLE users;
- capable enough for serious personal projects;
- efficient on ordinary consumer hardware;
- non-destructive and recoverable by default;
- local-first and privacy-friendly;
- open to community development;
- extensible enough to grow toward advanced filmmaking workflows.

## 5. What RayShot Is Not

RayShot is not initially trying to:

- replace professional Hollywood post-production suites;
- compete feature-for-feature with Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, or Final Cut Pro;
- make AI-generated video the core product;
- require cloud storage for every project;
- require a subscription to use the basic editor;
- solve every filmmaking workflow in its first release.

## 6. Core Principles

### Creativity first

The user should spend their attention on story, pacing, sound, and visual decisions rather than software mechanics.

### Progressive complexity

Basic tasks should be easy. Advanced controls should be available when needed rather than presented all at once.

### Local ownership

The user's original media and primary project remain under the user's control. Cloud services are optional additions rather than architectural dependencies for the core editor.

### Non-destructive editing

Editing should change project instructions and references, not destroy original media.

### Recoverability

Autosave, undo/redo, checkpoints, and project history should make experimentation safe.

### Open development

RayShot should be designed so that developers with expertise in media, GPU programming, UX, accessibility, platform support, and other areas can contribute.

### Technology follows the problem

AI, cloud rendering, and other advanced technologies should only be introduced when a validated user problem justifies them.

## 7. Long-Term Vision

RayShot can evolve from a simple local NLE into a broader filmmaking platform:

```text
Simple local editor
        ↓
Efficient media/proxy pipeline
        ↓
Advanced editing capabilities
        ↓
Optional cloud rendering
        ↓
Collaboration / sync
        ↓
Optional AI assistance
        ↓
Open filmmaking ecosystem
```

The long-term destination is not merely another editing application. It is an accessible foundation for people who want to make films without being blocked by software complexity, expensive hardware, or unnecessary operational overhead.

## 8. Success at the Vision Level

RayShot is succeeding when a person who previously abandoned professional video software can say:

> “I understood how to use it, my laptop handled it, and I actually finished my film.”
